## 1. Overall Model Comparison (RQ1)

The table below summarizes the classification performance of the three models tested on the augmented test set (**2,870 samples**).

| Model | Accuracy | Precision (Weighted) | Recall (Weighted) | F1-Score (Weighted) | Status |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :--- |
| **Random Forest (RF)** | **85.26%** | **85.52%** | **85.26%** | **85.25%** | **Best Classifier** |
| Decision Tree (DT) | 83.24% | 83.29% | 83.24% | 83.22% | Baseline Classifier |
| Support Vector Machine (SVM) | 67.28% | 69.97% | 67.28% | 66.71% | Low performance |

### Key Analysis Points for the Paper:
- **Random Forest Superiority**: The Random Forest ensemble outperforms the single Decision Tree by **2.02%** in accuracy. This is due to its bootstrap aggregating (bagging) mechanism, which reduces model variance and mitigates overfitting on the augmented noise features.
- **Support Vector Machine (SVM) Performance**: The RBF-kernel SVM struggled (67.28% accuracy) due to the highly overlapping synthetic decision boundaries generated by the Gaussian noise augmentation, making it difficult for the hyperplanes to separate the 11 classes cleanly.

## 2. Feature Importance Analysis

Using the Mean Decrease in Impurity (MDI) from the Random Forest classifier, we computed the relative importance of each water quality parameter:

| Feature | Importance Score | Rank |
| :--- | :---: | :---: |
| **Turbidity** | **0.3593** | 1 |
| **Temperature** | **0.3555** | 2 |
| **pH** | **0.2852** | 3 |

### Scientific Justification:
*   **Turbidity** has the highest relative importance score, suggesting it acts as a primary filter for distinguishing between species that thrive in high-clarity waters (like *tilapia* or *pangas*) versus bottom-dwellers or air-breathing species that tolerate turbid waters (like *koi*, *magur*, and *sing*).
*   **Temperature** is a close second, reflecting its critical role in regulating ectothermic metabolic rates in specific target species.
*   **pH** is highly clustered, acting as a mandatory baseline criteria (most species prefer neutral-to-slightly-alkaline ranges), leading to lower variance in classification split-decisions compared to turbidity and temperature.

## 5. Sample Prediction Cases (SHAP Explanations Context)

These test cases illustrate how the model recommends species under different pond conditions:

*   **Case 1: Neutral, Warm, Low Turbidity**
    *   *Input:* `pH = 7.2`, `Temp = 28°C`, `Turbidity = 5.0 NTU`
    *   *Prediction:* **pangas (93.5% confidence)**
    *   *SHAP Interpretation:* High temperature and low turbidity positively attribute to `pangas` suitability, as it prefers warm, stable waters.
*   **Case 2: Slightly Alkaline, Hot, Low Turbidity**
    *   *Input:* `pH = 8.5`, `Temp = 30°C`, `Turbidity = 3.5 NTU`
    *   *Prediction:* **pangas (84.0% confidence)**
    *   *SHAP Interpretation:* Highly warm water pushes the prediction toward `pangas`, with `koi` (10%) and `tilapia` (5%) as secondary options.
*   **Case 3: Neutral, Cool, Moderate Turbidity**
    *   *Input:* `pH = 6.5`, `Temp = 25°C`, `Turbidity = 7.0 NTU`
    *   *Prediction:* **tilapia (51.6% confidence)**
    *   *SHAP Interpretation:* Slower/cool temperature lowers primary confidence but yields a stable recommendation for `tilapia`, with `sing` (30%) as a secondary hardy candidate.
